**Citation:** Hamilton, Alexander. *A New Account of the East Indies, Being the Observations and Remarks of Capt. Alexander Hamilton, from the Year 1688 to 1723.* 2 vols. Edinburgh: John Mosman, 1727.
**Type:** PRIMARY source, English, published two years after my January 1725 purchase date. Hamilton was a Scottish private ship captain who traded in Asia, so this is an eyewitness account, and he had reasons to dislike the English company.

**How I used it:** I did a few targeted searches (Dutch, Banda, interloping) and read only those passages. I did not read the books.

## Snippets

1. **The Dutch and the Banda spice islands (vol. 2, about p. 11 to 12).** Hamilton writes that the islands of Banda, "where Cloves, Nutmegs, and Mace grow, but are now all engrossed by the Dutch, who allow one of them called Tullowey, to belong to the English, after they had been at 40 Years Pains to cut down all the Clove and Nutmeg Trees that grew on it, and have made it Death for the Natives ever to plant any on it."
   - Why it matters: an eyewitness-era claim from 1727 that the Dutch company enforced its spice monopoly with the death penalty for the local people. Smith (1776) says something similar but only as hearsay (notes/Smith_1776.md), and the VOC's own letters talk about uprooting clove trees (notes/generale_missiven.md).

2. **The English company's attitude to private traders (vol. 2, about p. 56, running head unclear in OCR).** Hamilton says he could not help a captain named Terrin because "I, having three or four large Ships at Bengal, was reckoned a Criminal guilty of that unpardonable Sin of Interlopping."
   - Why it matters: it shows how the EIC treated competitors. The company guarded its monopoly even against English traders, which supports Karak's point that the company defended its monopoly profits (notes/karak-2024.md).

## Gaps
- I only found two good passages with my searches. There is more on Batavia, Calcutta and slavery in the books, but the bad OCR made keyword searching unreliable and I did not want to spend more.
- Hamilton covers 1688 to 1723, so he is describing the world just before 1725, not 1725 to 1750.
- No share prices or dividends.

## How useful is this source?
Useful as a primary, period eyewitness source for how both companies behaved (Dutch monopoly enforcement, English hostility to interlopers). It is weak as evidence for investment returns.
