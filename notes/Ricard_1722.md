## What the source is about

A big handbook for merchants and bankers in Amsterdam: weights, money, duties, insurance, brokers, the Bank of Amsterdam, exchange. Chapter XIX (pp. 370 to 409) is about the Dutch East India Company (VOC): its history, how it was run, how it sold goods, and how its shares ("Actions") were traded and paid dividends. It has every VOC dividend from 1605 to 1720 and a list of 1720 bubble prices that includes the English East India Company. Ricard writes "cette année 1721" (p. 376), so this is what a well informed merchant knew a few years before my ancestor's January 1725 decision. He admires the VOC, so he is not neutral.

## Facts I can use

### How the VOC was organized
- Chartered "par les Lettres Patentes des Seigneurs les Etats Generaux des Provinces-Unies, en date du 20. Mars 1602." (p. 372) Translation: by letters patent of the States General, dated 20 March 1602. Private Dutch traders were banned beyond the Cape of Good Hope (p. 372).
- First capital 6,459,840 florins, about half from Amsterdam, the rest from Zeeland, Rotterdam, Delft, Hoorn and Enkhuizen (p. 373).
- Shareholders got no vote: "il fut résolu que les Directeurs qui l'étoient déjà, resteroient, & qu'ils auroient la direction de toute la Compagnie." (p. 373) Translation: the existing directors would stay and run the whole company. The reason given: a meeting of so many shareholders would cause "dissention" (p. 373).
- 65 directors: 18 Amsterdam, 12 Zeeland, 7 each for Delft, Rotterdam, Hoorn and Enkhuizen, plus a few from other towns (p. 374).
- Chambers: "Tous ces Directeurs ont chacun leur charge & leur office dans la Ville ou Comptoir de leur departement, que l'on nomme Chambre, comme Chambre d'Amsterdam, Chambre de Zeelande, Chambre de Delft, &c." (p. 374) Translation: each director works in the office of his city, called a Chamber.
- A director needed at least 6,000 florins of original capital. Amsterdam directors served "pour toute leur vie" (for life) with a pension of 1,000 ducatons (p. 374).
- 8 "principaux participans" (main shareholders) had "voix deliberative, mais non conclusive" (p. 374): they could speak but not decide.
- Heeren XVII: "Cette Assemblée, qui s'appelle l'Assemblée des Dix-sept, est composée de 17 Directeurs députez de toutes les Chambres" (p. 375). 8 from Amsterdam, 4 Zeeland, 1 each from the four small chambers, and the 17th rotating among them. It met three times a year, and the first meeting set the spice sales "& les repartitions que la Compagnie doit faire" (and the dividends to pay) (p. 375).
- Relationship to the state: the directors rule "sous l'authorité des Etats Généraux" (under the authority of the States General) (p. 370).
- Monopoly: "il n'y a qu'elle seule dans le Monde, qui possede les Iles où croissent la Muscade & la Fleur de Muscade, le Gerofle & la Canelle" (p. 376). Translation: it alone in the world owns the islands where nutmeg, mace, cloves and cinnamon grow. So it fixed nutmeg and clove prices itself, at 75 sols a pound "jusques au mois de Mars de cette année 1721" (p. 376).
- Goods were sold at two public auctions a year (spring, and October/November), paid through the Bank of Amsterdam (pp. 377 to 379).

### How shares worked and were traded in Amsterdam
- "ce fonds fut divisé dès le commencement en portions de 500 Livres de gros ou de 3000 florins chacune, & ce sont ces mêmes portions que l'on apelle Actions Capitales ou simplement Actions" (p. 397). Translation: the capital was divided from the start into portions of 500 pounds Flemish or 3,000 florins, called capital shares or just shares.
- "elles s'achètent & se vendent, soit comptant ou à terme, tantôt à haut & tantôt à bas prix selon les repartitions ou devidents qu'il y a lieu d'esperer que la Compagnie donnera" (p. 397). Translation: bought and sold for cash or future delivery, high or low depending on the dividends people expect.
- Transfer (pp. 397 to 398): the seller goes to the "Maison des Indes" (East India House), the bookkeeper fills in a printed transfer, the seller and a director sign. The buyer pays through the Bank of Amsterdam, then the seller signs a receipt. Until then "l'Acheteur ne peut disposer en aucune maniere de l'action, quoi qu'il l'ait effectivement payée" (p. 398): the buyer cannot use the share even though he paid.
- "Il en coûte 3 florins 18 sols pour chaque transport" (p. 398). Each transfer cost 3 florins 18 sols.
- VOC shares were also traded as options ("Primes") (p. 62). Ricard warns "il y a dans ce Commerce mille & mille tours de finesse, & même bien souvent de friponnerie" (p. 62): a thousand tricks, and often plain cheating.
- Government placards (pp. 403 to 410) banned selling shares you did not own and required sales to be registered in the company books within 14 days (same city) or a month (other city), with a fine of a quarter of the price (pp. 404, 406, 408).

### Dividends ("Repartitions")
- "On apelle Repartition & en Hollandois Uytdeeling ce que la Compagnie distribue & paye tous les ans à ceux qui ont quelque action dans la Compagnie" (p. 398). It is set by profits and returns from the Indies and always counted on the original 3,000 florin share, not the market price (p. 398).
- The VOC never made a capital call, "loin d'avoir jamais fait aucun appel" (p. 373), and paid 15% already in 1605.
- Full list on pp. 399 to 400. Some early dividends were paid in cloves, and in the 1680s and 1690s some were paid in "Obligations" (bonds) instead of cash. Dividends just before my period, all "en Argent" (cash) (p. 400):

| Year | Dividend (% of original capital) |
|---|---|
| 1700 | 25 |
| 1701 to 1702 | 20 each |
| 1703 to 1711 | 25 each |
| 1712 | 15 |
| 1713 | 30 |
| 1714 | 33 1/3 |
| 1715 to 1720 | 40 each |

- "Ce qui fait en tout 2602 2/3 pour cent, & revient à environ 22 1/8 pour cent par an du fonds Capital." (p. 400) Translation: 2602 2/3 percent in total, about 22 1/8 percent a year on the original capital.
- My math: at a price of about 850% (below), a 40% dividend is only about 4.7% a year on what a buyer actually paid. That is the figure that matters for my ancestor.

### Share prices around 1720 to 1722
- VOC: "Les Actions de la Compagnie des Indes de ces Pays, qui étoient à 1000. p. cent, ont été poussées à 1260. & sont retombées à 850." (p. 402) Translation: from 1000% up to 1260% and back to 850%.
- When he wrote: "à present que les Actions valent autour de 850 pour cent" (p. 374). So one 3,000 florin share cost about 25,500 florins.
- EIC: "Celles de la Compagnie des Indes d'Angleterre, qui valoient 198. ont été à près de 500. & sont retombées à 160 p. cent." (p. 402) Translation: from 198 to nearly 500, back to 160. (The OCR text says 158, the image shows 198.)
- South Sea Company: about 120 in early 1720, 1000 in July, 150 in January 1721. Bank of England: 148, to 300 and more, back to 130 (p. 402).
- He says the share trade "a ruiné un très grand nombre de familles dans l'année 1720" (ruined a great many families in 1720) (pp. 400 to 401) and praises Amsterdam, Leiden and Haarlem for refusing new bubble companies (p. 401).

### English companies
- "Les Actions de la Compagnie des Indes d'Angleterre, quoi qu'il soit défendu d'en negocier à Amsterdam." (p. 62) Translation: shares of the English East India Company (are traded), although it is forbidden to trade them in Amsterdam.
- Bank of England shares "se vendent, s'achètent & se transportent à peu près de la même maniere que les Actions des Compagnies des Indes Orientales & Occidentales à Amsterdam" (p. 502). Translation: are sold, bought and transferred in about the same way as Dutch company shares. The Bank lends "à l'Etat" (to the state) (p. 502).
- Nothing on how the EIC was governed or its dividends.

### Ethics
- Ricard excuses VOC force: if it armed against Eastern powers "c'a été plutôt pour conserver les droits que son Commerce lui avoit acquis, que dans la vue de faire de nouvelles Conquêtes" (p. 371). Translation: more to keep the rights its trade had won than to make new conquests. In the same passage he boasts merchants can "subjuguer des Pays, emporter des Villes, & gagner des Batailles" (subjugate countries, take cities and win battles) (p. 371). Good evidence of the European view, not proof the VOC was peaceful.
- The spice monopoly on Banda, Amboina and Ceylon (p. 376) needs to be linked to the violence used to get it, from other sources.
- Slaves: only in the West India Company chapter, about Suriname. The WIC alone could take "des Esclaves de la Côte d'Afrique" and sold them publicly "deux à deux" (two by two) (pp. 415 to 416). Nothing on slaves in the VOC chapter.
- Market ethics: placards say short sellers spread "des bruits aussi faux que desavantageux" (false and harmful rumours), hurting "des Veuves, des Orphelins" (widows and orphans) who had to sell cheap (p. 403).

## Gaps
- Stops in 1721. No prices or dividends for 1725 to 1750.
- Almost nothing on the EIC beyond its 1720 price swing (p. 402) and the Amsterdam ban (p. 62).
- Pro VOC author, very little criticism.
- Nothing on VOC debts or falling profits.

## How useful is this source? (my take)
High for the VOC side. It is a French primary source from just before the investment and explains how the VOC was run (65 directors, chambers, Heeren XVII, shareholders without a real vote), how a share was transferred and what it cost, and every dividend to 1720. The best part is p. 402, which puts the VOC and EIC side by side after 1720: a VOC share cost about 850% of face value and paid 40%, while EIC stock had fallen back to 160. Weak on the EIC and on 1725 to 1750, so I need other sources for those.
