**Type:** PRIMARY source in **Dutch**. The letters are the yearly reports the Governor-General and Council in Batavia sent to the company directors (Heren XVII). **Page numbers:** printed page numbers of the edition (the "p." markers in my text files).

**How I used it:** I did not read these volumes. They are thousands of pages. I searched by Dutch keywords (Chineesen, slaven, Madras, verval, winsten) and read only the passages around the hits. The quotes are copied from the OCR with line-break hyphens joined. **Translations are mine, so treat them as rough.**

## Snippets

### 1. Batavia, October 1740: the company's own words about the Chinese
- **Deel 10, p. 583** (letter of 10 January 1741, Valckenier, original text). The Council speaks of "het gespuys der rebelleerende Chineesen" (the scum of the rebelling Chinese). It also says Chinese women and children held in the jails were released, and that their slaves ("slaven") and "lijffeygenen" (bondspeople) could be reclaimed by owners who proved their title before the Court of Justice.
- **Deel 10, p. 587** (same letter, original text). The Council expects "maar een aantal van 7 a 8000 Chineesen" (only about 7 or 8,000 Chinese) in the rebel area and plans to use tents, axes and troops "tot verdelging van dat gespuys" (for the destruction of that scum).
- **Deel 10, p. 629** (letter of 25 March 1741, original text). The rebels claimed their revolt came from "de oppacking dier natie en derselver versending naar Ceylon" (the arrest of their people and shipping them to Ceylon) and from "het gerugt dat deselve onderweeg overboord zouden zijn geworpen" (the rumor that they would be thrown overboard on the way). The Council answers that this rumor was "verdigtselen ... verre afgeweken van de waarheyd" (inventions far from the truth).
- **Deel 10, p. 650** (running head says 6 November 1741, original text). A list of goods taken from Chinese junks that belonged to "gemassacreerde Chineesen" (massacred Chinese), to be shown to officials in Canton. So the company itself used the word massacred.
- **Why it matters:** this is the VOC's own record of Batavia 1740. It shows the company treated the massacre as a security matter and kept track of the property.

### 2. Slavery and forced labor
- **Deel 11, p. 35** (letter of 31 December 1743, original text, about Menado). Batavia says the rank of "hoekum major" had been banned in 1711, 1712 and 1722 as "seer pernicieux" (very harmful), and warns that it could be used "om den inlander bij uytnementheyd te drucken, ja selfs om vrije menschen tot slaven te maken, mitsgaders de dienaren van de Compagnie daarvan eygenaars te doen werden" (to oppress the native above all, even to make free people into slaves and make Company servants their owners). The Council ordered it suppressed again.
- **Deel 11, p. 28** (letter of 31 December 1743, original text, about Amboina). If the Company gets "overkropt" (overstocked) with cloves, "de inlanders gehouden sullen blijven een gedeelte derselve dan wel de helfte of meerder uyt te roeyen" (the natives shall be obliged to uproot part of their trees, or half or more). The editor's summary on the same page also approves the yearly "hongitochten" (armed tours to destroy unlicensed clove trees).
- **Deel 11, p. 40** (editor's summary in modern Dutch, Makassar). Batavia lets the local officials ease the rules because "men geen slaven tegen de vastgestelde prijs van 24 rsd kan verkrijgen" (slaves could not be bought at the set price of 24 rijksdaalders). That means the company itself was buying slaves at a fixed price.
- **Why it matters:** the central VOC government worried about its own servants abusing people, but it also ran forced spice cultivation and bought slaves. This supports the ethics section. The VOC was not a clean choice.

### 3. Money
- **Deel 11, p. 189** (editor's summary, modern Dutch, of the 31 December 1744 letter). For the company's Persian branch in 1741/42, costs were ƒ239,042 and profits ƒ201,428, so "De lasten hebben de winsten overtroffen met ƒ 37.595" (costs exceeded profits by ƒ37,595). It blames a 1740 decision to cut textile prices so far that almost all textiles sold at a loss.
- **Deel 11, p. VII** (editor's introduction, 1997). The letters under Governor-General Van Imhoff contain "zijn ideeen om het verval van de Verenigde Oostindische Compagnie tegen te gaan" (his ideas for stopping the decline of the VOC). This is a modern editor's judgment, not the 1740s company speaking.

## Gaps
- There are **no share prices or dividends** in these letters. They are about Asian operations, not the stock market.
- I found nothing in the 1743 to 1750 volume that clearly describes the French capture of Madras in 1746 or the Dutch entering the war in 1747. My "Madras" searches only hit unrelated mentions of English and French trade. I did not search deel 8 and 9 beyond a quick look.
- The p. 189 loss is one regional office, not the whole VOC, and it is an editor's summary. Do not cite it as proof the whole company lost money. For that, use the VOC finance paper (notes/VOCPAPERAPRIL231.md).

## How useful is this source? (my take)
Good for the ethics section and for the **Dutch primary source** requirement. For the investment question it only gives background. When I quote it in the paper, I should quote only the parts printed as original 18th century Dutch (snippets 1 and 2 from Deel 10 and Deel 11 pp. 28 and 35), not the editor's summaries.
